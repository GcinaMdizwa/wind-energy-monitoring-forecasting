01 / MONITORING

Weather, not telemetry

Open-Meteo provides numerical weather estimates at the selected grid point. Charts use hourly wind at 100 m; the current 10 m weather reading is a separate variable and is not mixed with those charts. Refreshes are cached for 10 minutes. The map shows the requested point, while the weather provider may return a nearby grid cell.

Forecast API documentation ↗
02 / ESTIMATION

Power has assumptions

Single-turbine illustrative cubic curve: cut-in 3 m/s, rated speed 12 m/s, cut-out 25 m/s. Rated capacity is adjustable. No air-density correction, wakes, outages, electrical losses or actual manufacturer curve. Energy sums hourly estimated power × one hour. These estimates are not bankable energy-yield assessments.

03 / LEARNING

RNN × LSTM

The hybrid combines parallel vanilla-RNN and LSTM encoders with a dense prediction head. Inputs are 48 hours of wind speed, circular wind direction, hour and day-of-year. It directly predicts 24 future hourly wind speeds. Single RNN and LSTM alternatives are available. Longer training history adds seasonal examples; it does not guarantee skill.

04 / VALIDATION

Future data stays in the future

Chronological 70% training, 15% validation and 15% test target periods. Scaling is fit only on training data. Early stopping uses validation loss. Reported MAE and RMSE compare the selected model with persistence across the same rolling test windows. Overlapping windows are correlated; one site's holdout is not proof of generalisation.

05 / COASTAL RESEARCH

Learning the coastal wind

Up to five years of ERA5 reanalysis can be retrieved at a selected land or offshore point. This version learns atmospheric wind, not waves, tides or sea-state dynamics. Coastal grid resolution and transfer from historical reanalysis to current forecast weather can introduce bias. Measured mast, buoy or turbine SCADA data and walk-forward validation are needed for operational use. Retraining is manual.
